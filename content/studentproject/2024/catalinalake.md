Catalina Lake is a gigantic ice-dammed lake on the Renland Peninsula in East Greenland. Every few decades, the glacier dam fails and billions of tonnes of freshwater are released into the fjord within a matter of days.

<!--more-->

The sudden disappearance of this water removes a substantial load from both the ice sheet and the underlying Earth. Does the surrounding landscape measurably rebound when the lake drains?

In this project, you will investigate whether this response can be detected from space. A recent drainage event was observed by modern satellites, providing a unique opportunity to compare observations with models of elastic deformation.

Possible project components include:

* Measuring surface deformation using Sentinel-1 InSAR observations.
* Estimating lake volume changes from satellite imagery and elevation data.
* Modelling the elastic response of the ice sheet and underlying Earth.
* Comparing observations and model predictions.
* Exploring whether observed deformation can constrain the mechanical properties of ice and bedrock.

Depending on your interests, the project can focus on satellite data analysis, physical modelling, or inverse methods.

The project will build on:

* This [Catalina Lake study](/publication/2017-09-06-catalina-lake/).
* Satellite observations from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2.
* Elastic deformation and load-response models.
